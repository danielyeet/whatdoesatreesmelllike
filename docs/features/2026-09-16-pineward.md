# Pineward

Date: 2026-09-16 (`52d6f76`, *Draw the contact sheet in specks, and write Pineward*),
its wood grown through the whole page in `8137578` and its parts made to open gradually
in `a02339d`, both 2026-09-17. Migrated from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-17.

Files: `houses/pineward.html`, `pineward.js` (~690 lines), the `pine-*` block in
`style.css`, `tests/pineward.spec.js`

## What it is

The first house in Scent descriptions, "the house that smells like trees", and the piece
with the most in it. A long page — an introduction and **fifty-four parts**, one per
fragrance, each a picture and the owner's own notes — and the layout is the answer to
that length.

The parts are in **alphabetical order by the fragrance's name**, and the writing in them
is the owner's: their spelling and punctuation are theirs, so don't tidy them. The
scores they originally carried ("9.5/10" at the end of an entry) were taken out at their
request; two are left where they are part of a sentence rather than a verdict on its
own.

## A part is compacted

Closed, a part is its number, a small picture and its title on a ruled line with a run
of dots between the title and the cue; open, it is the picture at size with the writing
beside it. Fifty-four of anything listed straight down a page is a wall.

It is a real `<details class="pine-part">`, so it opens and closes, takes the keyboard
and works without the script.

## Opening and closing a part

**A click that lands while a part is still moving is remembered, not dropped.** The
handler used to return and do nothing while an open or close was running, so opening a
part and changing your mind within the next three-quarters of a second swallowed the
click. Only one is kept, so hammering the summary does at most one more thing. ADAR had
the identical handler and the identical bug; see [its report](2026-09-17-adar.md) for how
it was found, which is a story about not trusting "it only fails under load".

**Opening and closing one is a movement, not a cut** (`OPEN_MS` 760ms, `SHUT_MS` 620ms,
`PART_EASE`). A `<details>` does both in one frame on its own, which is right when
nothing is watching and wrong here — the owner asked for both halves to be "smooth and
gradual". So the summary's own click is caught and the element is opened or closed
around an animation:

- **Opening**, the element is opened at once (its contents have to be on the page to be
  measured), the box travels from nothing to the height it wants, and the writing comes
  up a beat later so the room is already opening before anything appears in it.
- **Closing**, the writing goes first and the box follows it down, and only when the box
  has closed is the element really shut. Shut it first and the browser takes the
  contents off the page in that frame, which is the cut this exists to avoid.

**The padding travels with the height.** Left behind, the last frame of closing is a
forty-pixel box with nothing in it that then disappears — a step at the end of a smooth
movement. It is read off the stylesheet rather than written in the script, so the two
cannot disagree.

[ADAR](2026-09-17-adar.md)'s fragrances open exactly the same way, from the same code
written twice: these pages share nothing by design.

## The four strata

The parts are grouped into four **strata** — Canopy, Understorey, Trunk, Roots — a
section through a forest read from the light down into the ground. The first two carry
**fourteen** each and the last two **thirteen**, which is what fifty-four divides into
four ways; it was four of thirteen when the page was written and the owner's list came
to fifty-four. The strata and the numbers are in the page's own markup, not worked out
in the script.

## The wood

**The wood is grown, and it runs the length of the piece.** Conifers stand down both
margins of the whole page rather than as one canopy behind the title: each is a straight
leader carrying whorls of branches (`WHORLS`) that shorten as they rise and droop as
they reach out (`BRANCH_DROOP`), with specks strung along them (`TWIG_EVERY`) and only
their near neighbours joined (`treeAt`, `branchOf`, `growWood`, `paintWood`). The owner
asked for the trees "extended and go through the entire page" and made "more
coniferous".

The whole page's trees are grown ONCE, in the page's own coordinates, and bucketed into
rows so that only the rows on screen are drawn — growing them per screenful instead
would re-roll the wood every time you scrolled. It grows from nothing over `GROW_MS`
(2.1s) when the page opens and then holds its shape.

**Where a tree may stand, and how it is kept there.** The first version dropped a tree
every `TREE_EVERY` pixels down each side at a random offset from the edge, and gave each
one branches as long as its own height. The owner's notes on it were four, and all four
came from that: trees ran off the top and the foot of the page, they overlapped one
another, they reached across the writing, and they stood on the reading tree in the left
margin. What replaced it:

- **Two strips, worked out from the page.** Three things share this page's width — the
  reading tree in the left gutter, the writing in a column 940px wide down the middle,
  and the wood. The wood gets the strip between them on each side (`GUTTER_LEFT`,
  `COLUMN`, `COLUMN_PAD`, `GUTTER_RIGHT`), and a strip narrower than `BAND_MIN` carries
  **no wood at all** rather than a squashed one. On a window under about 1,100px there is
  genuinely no room for both, and none is drawn.
- **A tree is grown to fit its strip, not its height.** `treeAt` takes a `spread` — the
  furthest any branch of it may reach — and the strip caps it. This is what stops a
  branch crossing the writing.
- **Walked down, not dropped in.** Each strip is walked from `PAGE_PAD` to the foot, one
  tree at a time, each starting below the last one's lowest branch, its droop included.
  Two trees on a side therefore *cannot* overlap, and nothing hangs off either end of the
  page. Dropping them at fixed intervals and hoping is what produced the overlaps.
- **And they fade at the window's edges** (`EDGE_FADE`). Holding a tree inside the page
  is not enough on its own: one that straddles the top or foot of the *screen* is still
  cut off by it in a hard line, which is what read as the wood running off the page.
  Faded, a tree dissolves into the margin and is never seen sliced.

`CLEAR_MID` is still there and still quietens the middle of the page, but it is a second
guarantee behind the strips now rather than the only one.

**The web between the specks is short and capped** (`WEB_REACH` 26px, `WEB_EACH` 2).
Joining every speck within reach — the first version — came out as long lines striking
across the page and closing into triangles: a net thrown over the title rather than
something growing behind it. [The essay pages](2026-09-17-the-essay-pages.md) inherited
the small numbers from this mistake.

## It idles, and it blooms

`IDLE`, `IDLE_RATE`, `BLOOM_*`. A speck drifts about a pixel around its own place on its
own slow clock, so the wood is never quite still while never going anywhere — the owner
asked for exactly that: "the particles themselves are free to slightly idle, yet the
taught character should remain the same".

Near the pointer (within `BLOOM_REACH` — 104px, cut back from 150 when the owner asked
for the reactivity to be cleaner, and eased faster with it, `BLOOM_EASE`) a speck is drawn more plainly and puts
out a few short needles, eased in and out (`BLOOM_EASE`) so the bloom follows the hand
rather than switching on and off with it. **Nothing about the drawing moves with the
pointer** — it is the same tree in the same place, drawn fuller where the hand is —
which is the other half of what they asked for: "this should not be interactive and
should not move with the mouse".

## The reading tree, and the scale beside it

**The page's progress is the house's own mark.** The owner asked for it outright, in
capitals and with the bottle in front of them: the progress bar of this page *is* the fir
off the Pineward label. It stands in the left margin, drawn empty as an outline, and is
inked in from the ground up as the parts go past. It replaced a plain hairline rule with
the ticks hung on it.

The fir is **worked out rather than drawn by hand** (`firPath`): a tier at a time down a
straight leader, each reaching further out than the one above it (`FIR_REACH`) and
notching back in under itself (`FIR_NOTCH`), with a bare stem at the foot. Generating it
means the tiers fit whatever height the mark is given, and means the shape lives beside
the drawing rather than in a file nobody will find again. The fill is a clip rectangle
that grows upward; `y` and `height` are SVG geometry rather than layout, so the CSS
transition on them is a nicety where it is honoured and the mark simply steps where it is
not.

**The scale is still there beside it**, and should stay: one tick per part, inked in as
it is passed, kept fine so that the tree is what is read and the count is there to be
looked for. `tests/pineward.spec.js` counts those ticks.

**Both read the PARTS and not the scrollbar** — a part that runs long should not read as
more of the piece than a part that runs short — and the whole thing is taken off the page
below 860px, where the window is the whole of the room.

The reading names what fills the window rather than what was passed last (`NAMES_IT`,
0.45), the same way ADAR's does; the two are worth keeping in step.

## Without the script

The page is all of its writing. The trunk and the reading are added by the script and
the wood is drawn by it; none of them carry anything to read.

## The one tree on a phone

The wood stands in the two strips between the reading gutter and the writing's column. On
a phone the writing takes the whole width of the window and the gutters take what is left,
so **both strips come out narrower than a tree and this page had no wood on it at all**
below about a thousand pixels across.

The owner asked for one of them on the phone too, and marked where they wanted it: the top
right of the head, in the clear band above the piece's own kicker. So when there is no
strip to stand in, one tree stands there — grown by the same `treeAt` as every other tree
here, and blooming under the hand like any of them (measured: 2,727 pixels of ink at rest,
4,945 under the pointer, 2,728 when it goes again). The page is asked where its writing
starts rather than told, and the tree's lowest branches have to droop clear of it or it is
not drawn at all: a tree hanging into the kicker is the one thing this must not do.

**Two of the wood's own rules are turned off for it, and both for the same reason.**
Quietening across the middle of the page exists to keep the wood off the writing, and a
tree standing in an empty band has no writing beside it. And the fade at the window's top
edge exists so a tree straddling the edge of the screen is never seen sliced — at
`EDGE_FADE`'s 130px that would have half-drawn a tree standing exactly where it was asked
to stand, so this one fades over `ONE_FADE`, 70.

`ONE_TALL`, `ONE_ROOF`, `ONE_CLEAR`, `ONE_WIDE` and `ONE_FADE` in `pineward.js`, and
`wood.clearing` is what `lit()` asks.

## How to test it

```bash
npm test -- tests/pineward.spec.js
```

Eight tests: the piece being an introduction and fifty-four parts in four strata of
fourteen, fourteen, thirteen and thirteen, numbered straight through in the markup; a
part being a title and a small picture until it is opened and its picture and writing
once it is; the trunk carrying one tick per part with the reading counting what has been
passed and letting go again on the way back up; the wood running the length of the piece
and being grown when the page opens; it idling where it stands and blooming under the
hand; the piece being simply there with animation turned off; all of its writing being
there without its script. `tests/contact-sheet.spec.js` checks the sheet's first picture
points at it.

## The tree scrapped, and the green

The round after the reading tree went in, the owner scrapped it: *"remove the tree on the
left, scrap that idea."* It is out of the file rather than switched off — there is no
`firPath` and no `FIR_*` in `pineward.js` any more, and no `.pine-tree` in the
stylesheet. What stands there now is the scale it replaced, given the job the tree had:
a hairline the length of the piece, **filled in dark green behind the ticks** as far as
you have read.

### It has to reach the end

The owner's other note on it: *"if I scroll all the way down, then it doesn't do that"* —
the reading stopped short of the full count. The cause is worth writing down, because it
is not obvious and it will come back if the rule is changed.

A part counts as passed when its top crosses a line **a third of the way down the
window**. The last few parts never get that far up the screen: the page runs out before
they can, so scrolling to the very bottom left the reading at about fifty of fifty-four
and the rule short of its end. The fix is a second rule, not a different line: **once the
page itself has been passed, so has everything on it** — when the scroll is at its
maximum, every part counts. Both the ticks and the fill come off that same count, so they
reach their end together.

### Where the green is spent

`--pine-green` (`#1a4a2c`), scoped to `.pineward-page`, is this page's only accent and no
other page on the site spends it. It is on four things, and they are all the same thing —
*where you are*: the scale's fill, the ticks behind you, the reading in the corner, the
number of the stratum you are in, and the cue on a part that is open or under the hand.

**The wood answers the hand in green too**, which the owner asked for as an addition to
the bloom rather than a replacement for it: a speck near the pointer is drawn more
plainly, carried `GREEN_LIFT` of the way towards the green, and puts out its needles, all
on the one eased value. It is deliberately **not** carried all the way to green — at 1.0
the wood reads as a different drawing under the pointer rather than as the same one
answering. `pineward.js` keeps its own copy of the colour as `GREEN`, because a canvas
cannot read a CSS custom property; change one and change the other.

## The wood is brown and green

The owner asked for green accents on this page and then, a round later, for the wood to
be drawn in its own two colours: *"let the 'bark' to be brown, and the 'leaves' and
'branches' of these trees to be green. The lines that appear should form a gradient where
the closer they are to the actual bark, they should take its colour, and as they get
closer to the branches, they should be more green."*

So every speck and every length of branch in the canopy carries a **`green` of its own** —
0 for bark, 1 for foliage — and one `tone()` mixes `BARK` (`#4a3422`) and `GREEN`
(`#1a4a2c`). One place decides the wood's colour rather than three.

| | `green` |
|---|---|
| the leader, and the specks up it | 0 — bark the whole way |
| a length of branch | `0` at the trunk to `1` at the tip |
| a needle on a branch | `0.5 + 0.5 ×` how far out it stands |
| a line of the web | what the two specks it joins average to |

**The gradient is made of the branch's own length**, not of where it is on the page, so it
comes out right on every tree whatever size that tree happened to be grown. And the
needles start well along towards green rather than at 0: running them 0→1 like the branch
does turns the inner needles into bark, which they are not.

The hand still greens the wood where it rests — `GREEN_LIFT` now carries a speck the rest
of the way to green from wherever its own colour already is, so the bloom is on top of
the colour rather than instead of it.

`--pine-bark` is in the stylesheet beside `--pine-green`; `pineward.js` keeps the same
pair as `BARK` and `GREEN`. Change one and change the other. `rgbaGreen()`, `INK`,
`INK_RGB` and the wood's `rgba()` are gone — nothing in the canopy is drawn in ink any
more.

Fifty-two of them for fifty-two parts, once two parts were removed — so every part on this

Ten were matched across a spelling rather than exactly: *Brokilän* / `Brokilan`, *Delfiño*

*Ivy Moss* / `Ivymoss`, *Mint Cocoa* / `Mint cocoa`, *Snoqualmie* / `snoqualime`,

**Fanghorn I and Gelatto are gone**, at the owner's word, and the parts renumber straight

> Note: Fanghorn (the first version) also existed at some point, but I did not try it

The renumbering is the thing to be careful of here. The numbers are **in the markup**, not
counted, which is deliberate — they are the owner's — so removing a part means rewriting
every number after it, the `id` each part is linked by, and the four strata's ranges.
There is a test that they run 01 to 52 unbroken.

## The ground is pine green

The owner: *"try to incorporate some background elements in pine green colours"*. Three
things, all of them the page's ground rather than anything over the reading: the paper
turned 2% towards the green, two soft pools of it high on each side where the wood stands,
and the green gathering towards the foot of the page — which is the Roots stratum, so the
page's ground reads down from the light into the earth along with its writing.

It has to stay that faint. This page's accent is worth something *because* it is spent in
so few places; a green page would spend it everywhere. Built with `color-mix()`, with a
`@supports` fallback to the plain paper for a browser without it.

## The owner's own groupings, and the list of what is not smelled

Canopy, Understorey, Trunk and Roots were a section through a forest — a made-up
arrangement, and a good-looking one. The owner sent a spreadsheet saying which fragrance
is actually which, so the groups are **Foresty, Hay-y, Wine/Christmas, Sea and Others**
and the membership is theirs. The counts are uneven (14, 6, 4, 4, 19) because they are
however many happen to smell of that; there is nothing to balance.

The sections are `id="stratum-1"` … `stratum-5` rather than named ids. The names are the
owner's to change and an id that is one of them would go stale the first time they did.

**A fragrance that has not been smelled is not a part.** Eight of them are a list of names
at the foot of the page — no number, no picture, no writing, because there is nothing yet
to number, show or write — and they move up into a group the moment there is something to
say. Borealis carries the owner's own line about it. The list is `.pine-waiting`, it
carries the groups' own head so it reads as one more group, and there is a test that it
contains no pictures and no `.pine-part`: if it ever did, the trunk down the side would
tick for a fragrance that does not exist yet.

**Renumbering is the expensive part of this page** and it is expensive on purpose. The
numbers are in the markup rather than counted, because they are the owner's — so taking a
fragrance out means rewriting every number after it, the `id` each part is linked by, the
five groups' ranges, and **every anchor in the Fragrances index**. That last one is the
trap; see [the index's report](2026-09-17-the-index-pages-and-views.md).

## The spelling, fixed once

The owner asked for the capitalisation and the basic spelling to be fixed. Every change is
the same word, spelled — `semlls`, `hte`, `pne pf`, `courtsey`, `overwheming`,
`tourquoise` and sixty-odd others — plus missing apostrophes, a capital at the start of a
sentence, and `AdV` written out as Aedes De Venustas.

**Their voice is not spelling.** `idk`, `ngl`, `istg`, `lmao`, `v wet`, `hehehehe`, the
run-on sentences and the notes to themselves are all still exactly as written. If a fix
ever has to be made again, the map is in the commit that made this one; the rule is that a
correction turns a word into *the same word*, never into a better one.

**One thing was left alone and should be looked at**: Gingermoss and Hayride both carry
Acadian's writing word for word. That reads as a copy-paste when the page was first built
rather than as anything the owner meant, and inventing writing for two fragrances is not a
spelling fix — so it stands, and it is flagged here.

## The bloom holds for a quarter of a second

The owner asked for *"a delay to the effects of the pineward trees, so that when you hover
a tree, the effect lasts about 0.25 seconds after your cursor leaves"*.

**The wait is per speck, and that is the whole of why it reads right.** Leaving the *page*
already faded, because `handAt` eased down over about three tenths of a second. What
snapped was moving the pointer somewhere else **on** the page: the bloom is worked out
from how near the hand is to each speck, so a tree went out in the same frame the pointer
left it even though the hand was still there and `handAt` was still 1.

So every speck now takes a brighter bloom the moment it is offered one and starts its hold
again; a dimmer one is refused until `BLOOM_HOLD` has run out, and then eased down rather
than dropped. Rising is at once, falling waits. The tree you have just left stays lit
behind you and fades.

It costs two numbers per speck and no extra work per frame, which matters on a page that
was optimised for phones the same week.

## The notes panel

Each of the forty-seven carries a VIEW NOTES button now — see [the notes
report](2026-09-21-the-notes.md). **Pineward publishes no pyramids at all**: neither its
own Master Scent List nor Fragrantica divides a single one of them into top, mid and base,
so every entry for this house is a flat list and the panel says so. Forty-six of the
forty-seven are filled in; Glühwein is the one still wanted.
