# View notes, and the Fragrances page it arrived with

Date: 2026-09-21

Files: `notes.js` (~210 lines, new), `notes-data.js` (new), the `note-*` and `human-house`
rules in `style.css`, `works/individual-fragrances.html` (new), the Fragrances view of
`categories/scent-descriptions.html`, the five pages that load the notes
(`works/pineward.html`, `adar.html`, `almost-human.html`, `grande-parfums.html`,
`individual-fragrances.html`), `search-page.js`, `tests/notes.spec.js` (new).

## What it is

Two things the owner asked for together.

**Every fragrance now carries a VIEW NOTES button** at the foot of its writing — in the
site's mono rather than its reading face, boxed, with the hollow square this site uses
for anything that opens. Pressing it opens a panel **beside the writing**, on the right,
carrying the Top, the Mid and the Base, and the **source** underneath.

**And the Fragrances view of Scent descriptions is a different page.** It used to be an
index of every fragrance on the whole site, pointing back into the houses. The owner
asked for it to stop being that: *"an independent fragrance review page where I put
information about perfumes that do not belong in any house on the houses tab"*. The table
now holds seven, and they open into `works/individual-fragrances.html`, which is shaped
like a house so it gets the parts, the rank and the notes panel for nothing.

## A pyramid is not always a pyramid

This is the whole of the difficulty, and the owner saw it coming — *"unless the source
states that there is no division between top mid and base"*.

Plenty of houses publish **one undivided list**. Pineward is one: neither its own Master
Scent List nor Fragrantica divides a single one of its forty-seven. Serge Lutens does not
divide De Profundis. Dior does not divide Tobacolor. Almost Human goes further and says
so out loud — it sets its fragrances out as an *olfactory landscape* rather than a
pyramid, and Fragrantica follows.

So an entry is **one shape or the other**, never both, and the panel says which:

| | |
|---|---|
| `{ top, mid, base }` | only when the source actually divides them. Three rows. |
| `{ flat }` | one row, under a line saying *the source gives one list, undivided*. |
| no entry at all | the button is still there, and the panel says the notes have not been found yet. |

**The missing ones keep their button on purpose.** The alternative is no button, and then
a house with half its notes missing looks finished. What is missing should be visible —
and `notes.js` also says in the console which numbers a page is still waiting for.

### The rule that matters, and the near miss that set it

**Never write a pyramid a source did not state.** A review's prose is not a pyramid.

That is in `notes-data.js` in as many words because it nearly went wrong on the first
fragrance looked up. A search for Haxan's notes came back with a confident Top / Heart /
Base — *"opens with a relatively dry cedar wood and woody vetiver… the heart helps to
provide a beautifully warm creaminess"* — which had been assembled out of reviewers'
impressions on Basenotes. The actual source gives **forty notes and no division at all**.
Recorded as it came back, that would have been a fabricated pyramid on a perfume site,
attributed to a source that says the opposite.

The same thing happened again with Dior's Tobacolor and with Grande's Hot Stuff. Both are
flat or missing here rather than the pyramid the search offered.

## Where the data lives, and why not in the markup

`notes-data.js`, one entry per fragrance, keyed by the page's own `window.HOUSE_NOTES`
and the part's number — `"pineward:01"`, `"individual:06"`. There are eighty-odd of them
and the markup is the owner's to edit; ninety hand-written panels in five files would be
ninety places to get the numbering wrong.

It does mean the numbering is in two places. **Renumbering a house means renumbering here
in the same turn**, exactly as it means re-pointing the Fragrances table — and there is a
test for both ends of that.

## How much of it is filled in, and the thing that limited it

**73 of the 84 fragrances**: 17 pyramids and 56 flat lists.

| | filled | of |
|---|---|---|
| Pineward | 46 | 47 |
| ADAR | 6 | 11 |
| Almost Human | 5 | 5 |
| Grande Parfums | 10 | 15 |
| Individual fragrances | 6 | 7 |

**The sources themselves could not be opened.** This environment's network policy denies
`fragrantica.com`, `pinewardperfume.com`, `adarperfumes.com`, `parfumo.com`,
`basenotes.com` and `dior.com` at the gateway — every one of them answers 403 to CONNECT,
whether fetched by the browser tool or by curl. So everything here came through **web
search summaries** of those pages rather than from the pages themselves.

That is worth knowing before trusting or extending this file. It is why each entry names
its source with a link the owner can check, why anything that looked like a reviewer's
impression was left out, and why eleven are still missing: for those, the only thing to
be found was somebody's account of how the fragrance smells.

The eleven still wanted: Pineward's **Glühwein**; ADAR's **Aetherealism**, **Against All
Odds**, **Root Code**, **Lignum Dei** and **Lithos Diaphanes**; Grande's **Cookie
something (?)**, **Hot Stuff**, **Karak & Shisha**, **Vintage Memoir** and **White
Label**; and the individual fragrance the owner's own list skipped.

## Two names, corrected the way the house spells them

The same rule Grande Parfums' fragrances were given: a product's name is the house's, not
a typo to preserve. **99CV** is **CV99** at Lussur, and **Tobacolour** is **Tobacolor** at
Dior. Both are flagged to the owner.

## The picture gives way, not the writing

A part is a row — picture, writing — in a column 940px wide. Put a 252px panel in it and
something has to be narrower.

The first go let it be the writing, which came out **about 110px across: two words to a
line**. So `notes.js` puts a `notes-on` class on the part while its panel is open, and the
stylesheet takes the room off the **plate** instead. With the panel shut every one of
those rules is off and the part is exactly what it was.

Below 860px the row is a column anyway, so the panel goes under the writing and the plate
takes its own width back.

## How to test it

```bash
npm test -- tests/notes.spec.js
```

Nine tests, and **the four that matter most do not open a browser at all**. Eighty-odd
hand-gathered entries can be quietly wrong in ways nobody would notice, so: no entry is
both a pyramid and a flat list (which would claim both that the source divided the notes
and that it did not); every entry names a source with an `https` link; no entry is empty;
and **every key points at a part that really exists on its page**, which is the notes'
end of the renumbering trap.

The rest: the Fragrances view being its own page and pointing at no house; the button
appearing on every fragrance and opening a panel **to the right of the writing**, measured
rather than assumed; a flat list saying it is undivided and showing one row; a missing one
saying so; and no button at all with the script blocked.

## Known issues / TODO

- **Eleven fragrances still have no notes**, listed above. The panel and the console both
  say which.
- **The notes need JavaScript.** Everything else on a house page is readable without it;
  this is the one part that is not. It is reference beside the reading rather than the
  reading, which is why that was thought acceptable — but it is a real exception to the
  site's rule and worth revisiting if the list ever stops growing.
- **The sources are unreachable from this environment**, so nothing here was read at its
  source. Anything that matters should be checked against the link in its entry.
- **`window.HOUSE_NOTES` is a third thing to set** when a house is added, beside the
  search manifest and the contact sheet. Ataraxia and Les Abstraits deliberately do not
  set it: their fragrances have no names yet, so there is nothing to look up.
