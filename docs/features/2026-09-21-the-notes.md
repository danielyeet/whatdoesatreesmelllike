# View notes, and the Fragrances page it arrived with

Date: 2026-09-21

Files: `notes.js` (~460 lines), `notes-data.js`, the `note-*` and `human-house`
rules in `style.css`, `works/individual-fragrances.html` (new), the Fragrances view of
`categories/scent-descriptions.html`, the five pages that load the notes
(`works/pineward.html`, `adar.html`, `almost-human.html`, `grande-parfums.html`,
`individual-fragrances.html`), `search-page.js`, `tests/notes.spec.js` (new).

## What it is

Two things the owner asked for together.

**Every fragrance now carries a VIEW NOTES button** at the foot of its writing — and
Almost Human's five carry an **OLFACTORY LANDSCAPE** button before it, because that house
publishes one instead of notes — in the
site's mono rather than its reading face, boxed, with the hollow square this site uses
for anything that opens. Pressing it opens a **window over the page** carrying the Top,
the Mid and the Base, and the **source** underneath — a house's own page wherever one
exists, and Fragrantica, with a caution on it, only where none does.

**And the Fragrances view of Scent descriptions is a different page.** It used to be an
index of every fragrance on the whole site, pointing back into the houses. The owner
asked for it to stop being that: *"an independent fragrance review page where I put
information about perfumes that do not belong in any house on the houses tab"*. The table
now holds seven, and they open into `works/individual-fragrances.html`, which is shaped
like a house so it gets the parts, the rank and the notes panel for nothing.

## A pyramid is not always a pyramid

This is the whole of the difficulty, and the owner saw it coming — *"unless the source
states that there is no division between top mid and base"*.

Plenty of houses publish **one undivided list**. Pineward is one: neither its own Master
Scent List nor Fragrantica divides a single one of its forty-seven. Serge Lutens does not
divide De Profundis. Dior does not divide Tobacolor. Almost Human goes further and says
so out loud — it sets its fragrances out as an *olfactory landscape* rather than a
pyramid, and Fragrantica follows.

So an entry is **one shape or the other**, never both, and the panel says which:

| | |
|---|---|
| `{ top, mid, base }` | only when the source actually divides them. Three rows. |
| `{ flat }` | one row, under a line saying *the source gives one list, undivided*. |
| no entry at all | the button is still there, and the panel says the notes have not been found yet. |

**The missing ones keep their button on purpose.** The alternative is no button, and then
a house with half its notes missing looks finished. What is missing should be visible —
and `notes.js` also says in the console which numbers a page is still waiting for.

### The rule that matters, and the near miss that set it

**Never write a pyramid a source did not state.** A review's prose is not a pyramid.

That is in `notes-data.js` in as many words because it nearly went wrong on the first
fragrance looked up. A search for Haxan's notes came back with a confident Top / Heart /
Base — *"opens with a relatively dry cedar wood and woody vetiver… the heart helps to
provide a beautifully warm creaminess"* — which had been assembled out of reviewers'
impressions on Basenotes. The actual source gives **forty notes and no division at all**.
Recorded as it came back, that would have been a fabricated pyramid on a perfume site,
attributed to a source that says the opposite.

The same thing happened again with Dior's Tobacolor and with Grande's Hot Stuff. Both are
flat or missing here rather than the pyramid the search offered.

## Where the data lives, and why not in the markup

`notes-data.js`, one entry per fragrance, keyed by the page's own `window.HOUSE_NOTES`
and the part's number — `"pineward:01"`, `"individual:06"`. There are eighty-odd of them
and the markup is the owner's to edit; ninety hand-written panels in five files would be
ninety places to get the numbering wrong.

It does mean the numbering is in two places. **Renumbering a house means renumbering here
in the same turn**, exactly as it means re-pointing the Fragrances table — and there is a
test for both ends of that.

### What "said nothing" means, and why it is a shape of its own

There are now **three** things an entry can be, not two:

| | |
|---|---|
| a **pyramid** | `{ top, mid, base }`, only when the source divides them |
| a **flat list** | `{ flat: [...] }`, when the source gives one undivided list |
| **`missing`** | one sentence saying what was found, which was nothing |

And a fourth state that is not an entry at all: **no key**, which the window answers with
"The notes for this one have not been found yet."

The difference matters and the owner drew it themselves. *"For ADAR, use notes listed in
the prose itself for each of the fragrances. If you cannot find any, write 'no
information as of yet.'"* and, separately, *"For cookie something just say i couldnt find
this fragrance online."* Two different sentences for two different situations:

- **Root Code** and **Lithos Diaphanes** say *No information as of yet.* — and still name
  ADAR's own page as the source, because the house's page was read and simply never names
  a material.
- **Cookie something (?)** says *I could not find this fragrance online.* and names no
  source, because there was nothing to name. It is the one entry in the file with no
  source, and the test that requires one has that single exemption written into it.

## Which version, said in the window

The owner asked for it in as many words: *"For the pinewards with more than two versions,
make sure it is emphasized which versions notes youre adding on the website itself; and
add the notes of the most recent version."*

So an entry may carry a **`version`**, and where it does, that is the **first thing in the
window** — a boxed line above the notes, not a sentence in the aside underneath them. It
qualifies everything below it, so it goes above everything below it, and there is a test
whose real assertion is that ordering.

| | version carried | and what changed |
|---|---|---|
| Glühwein | 2025 Edition | More red champaca, a finer cocoa absolute, and storax, tolu, rose and sandalwood that the 2021 has none of. |
| Gingerbread | 2025 Icing Edition | Already the most recent. |
| Velvetine | 2025 revision | A complete rework — new cypress, a custom vintage Mousse de Saxe base, Mysore sandalwood and dragon's blood resin. |
| De Profundis | 2011 original | **Not** the most recent, and deliberately: the owner said *"De profundis make it the 2011 original"*. |
| Flamenco EDP | 2017 eau de parfum | And the name on the page now carries EDP, which the owner asked for. |

**A version has to name a year**, and the test says so. "The new one" ages out of being
true; a year does not, and a year is what the houses print.

**Fanghorn II is not on that list, and is not a version.** The house made it to replace
the original Fanghorn after one of the key ingredients became unavailable, so they are two
fragrances rather than two editions. Its aside says so. It also came off the fallback this
round: the owner said to use Fragrantica *if* the official page had nothing, and the
official page had it after all, on the Master Scent List.

## The olfactory landscape, which is a second window

Almost Human publishes no notes. It says so: it works in **olfactory landscapes**, five
impressions rather than a list of materials. For one round that meant its five fragrances
carried Fragrantica's reading and an aside explaining why.

The owner asked for both: *"add an 'olfactory landscape' button before the notes. It will
be the exact same as notes, but it will feature the olfactory landscape listed on the
almost human website. The notes themselves should come from fragrantica."*

So those five fragrances have **two buttons and two windows**:

| | |
|---|---|
| **OLFACTORY LANDSCAPE** | the house's own five impressions, sourced from `almosthuman.store`. It is never called a list of notes, because it is not one. |
| **VIEW NOTES** | Fragrantica's reading of the same fragrance, cautioned as every fallback is. |

| | the house's landscape |
|---|---|
| Burning Bridges | burning silence, glowing dust, cracked ground, dry heat, clear light ahead |
| Dear Future | soft ozone, glowing skin, digital warmth, soft amber light, distant calm |
| Desert Hope | sun-burnt sand, dry woods, resin heat, rose dust, distant cool light |

**A landscape can only ever come from the house**, and there is a test saying so. It is the
thing the house publishes *instead* of notes; a landscape on the fallback's authority
would be the fallback inventing the one thing it does not have.

### What it cost the script

This is the first thing on the site where one fragrance has two windows, and it is why
`notes.js` has a **`makeWindow`** now. Everything that used to be written once per
fragrance — the button, the panel, `open`, `close`, `settle`, the collapse listeners — is
written once per **window**, and the two share nothing but the page they stand over.

**One at a time still holds, and it had to be checked.** The scrim covers the page while
either window is up, so a *pointer* cannot reach the other button — but the keyboard can
still tab to it and press it. The test takes that path deliberately, and the guard in
`open()` is what catches it.

## Haxan has two halves

Asked for by hand: *"I would like you to double the size of the notes listed, where the
upper part is the owners account, and then the bottom half is the 'interpreted notes'
sourced from fragrantica... No note pyramid."*

So Haxan's window carries **two lists, two headings and two sources**: the perfumer's own
account above (25 materials, from PRIN), and Fragrantica's interpreted notes below (39).
Neither is a pyramid, because the fragrance is not published as one.

**The 39 were read off the owner's own screenshot of the page**, which makes this the one
entry on the site whose fallback list was *seen* rather than reported through a search
summary. Given that the sources cannot be reached from here at all, that is the most
reliable list in the file.

Only the lower half is cautioned, which is the caution doing its job: the two sources sit
one above the other and the reader can see exactly which one carries the warning.

## Two names, corrected the way the house spells them

The same rule Grande Parfums' fragrances were given: a product's name is the house's, not
a typo to preserve. **99CV** is **CV99** at Lussur, and **Tobacolour** is **Tobacolor** at
Dior. Both are flagged to the owner.

## The notes go with the fragrance

Collapse a fragrance and its window goes with it, and opening the fragrance again leaves
it shut — it has to be asked for again. The owner asked for exactly that.

**It takes two listeners, at two different moments.** The **click** on the summary is when
the reader asked for the fragrance to close, and the part's own script then takes most of
a second to run the box down; without this the window would stand over a page rearranging
itself behind it. The **toggle** is the safety net, for a part closed any other way — and
since the window went modal that is the one that actually fires, because with a window up
the page behind it cannot be pressed at all.

The one easy thing to get wrong is the third state: reopening the fragrance must **not**
bring the notes back. A window that remembered would read as the page deciding for you.
There is a test that walks all three.

## It is a window, and that is the owner's second word on it

It opened **beside the writing** at first — a third column in the part's own row — with a
separate bottom-sheet arrangement for phones. The owner asked for a window instead: *"I
WANT YOU to make it a popup window actually."*

**That turned out to be the simpler thing as well as the asked-for one.** Standing in the
row, the panel had to be paid for out of somebody's width: a part is a picture and a
column of writing inside 940px, and a 252px panel in there left the writing **about 110px
across — two words to a line** until the plate was told to give way. A window over the
page costs the layout nothing at all. So all of that is gone: no `notes-on` class, no
narrowed plate, no `flex` overrides on three different houses' text, and no second
arrangement for a phone.

**One window, sized against the viewport rather than by a media query.** `min(430px,
calc(100vw - 36px))` and `min(74vh, 640px)` mean a phone and a desktop get the same
window at the size each has room for; below 560px it takes a little more of the screen
and a little less padding, which is the only concession to width anywhere in it.

Three things it gained by leaving the row:

- **It says which fragrance it belongs to.** A window standing over the page has left its
  fragrance behind; the column beside the writing never had to worry about that. The name
  is in the bar, beside the word NOTES.
- **The bar is sticky**, because a flat list can be forty notes deep — Haxan's is.
- **It is really a dialog**: `role`, `aria-modal`, `aria-labelledby`, the close takes the
  keyboard when it opens and hands it back to the button when it shuts, escape closes it,
  and the page behind is held still and cannot be pressed.

**It lives on the `<body>`, and it has to.** A `position: fixed` element inside an
ancestor carrying a `transform` is positioned against that ancestor rather than against
the window — and a part's own box is given a transform while it opens. Left where it was
built, the window would have been fixed to the inside of a `<details>`.

## The source is the house's own, and Fragrantica is only the fallback

The owner set the order out in as many words, twice over in one message: *"i want you to
do this as a hierarchy sources; first the official page of that fragrance, and only if
that fails, fragrantica"*, and then, in capitals, *"ALWAYS THE SOURCE OF THE PERFUME
ITSELF and only then fragrantica"*.

So every entry was gone back over and re-sourced where a house's own list could be found
for it. Where it came off Pineward's own list, the entry says so and links to the page it
is on:

There are **89 sources in the file**, not 84, because an entry can carry more than one:
Haxan names two and Almost Human's five name a landscape's source beside their notes'.
Every one of them is counted here and every one of them is checked by the same test.

| source | how many |
|---|---|
| Pineward, Master Scent List | 24 |
| ADAR Perfumes | 9 |
| Pineward (a fragrance's own product page) | 6 |
| Almost Human | 5 |
| PRIN (Prin Lomros) | 1 |
| Fragrantica | 44 |

**45 of the 89 come from the house that made the perfume**, against 44 on the fallback —
which means the house's own pages now outnumber Fragrantica for the first time. It was
32 against 41 a round ago. What moved it: ADAR's own prose turned out to carry five more
fragrances than had been found, Glühwein and Velvetine came off Pineward's own pages

The rest are on the fallback because the house publishes no list at all — several of
these houses simply do not print their notes anywhere, which is the whole reason
Fragrantica exists.

Two of those source names are worth explaining. **Pineward, Master Scent List** is
`pinewardperfume.com/pages/master-scent-list`, one page carrying the notes for most of
the house at once — the owner named it themselves and asked for it listed where it was
used. **Pineward** on its own is a fragrance whose notes are on its own product page and
not on that list. They are named apart rather than collapsed together because they are
two different pages and the link has to go to the right one.

### The caution, and why it is only on the fallback

Hovering the word **Fragrantica** raises a small dark box saying *Fragrantica's notes are
not to be trusted as 100% fact.* — the owner's own sentence, set as their own line.

It is a `CAUTION` table in `notes.js` keyed by source name, not an `if` on the string
"Fragrantica", so a second untrustworthy source is one line rather than a rewrite. The
name carries `aria-describedby` at it, so a screen reader is told the caution whether or
not anything is hovered, and the box itself is `role="tooltip"`, shown on `:hover` and on
`:focus` — the second because the name is a link, and a keyboard reaches it.

**It stands above the source line rather than below it.** The source is the last thing in
the window, and the window scrolls its own contents; a box hanging below would have been
cut off by that scroll on every entry. The pointer triangle under it is a rotated square
on `::after`.

**The one rule here is that the caution is not on everything.** A house-sourced entry
carries none, and there is a test whose real assertion is that one — put the warning on
all 73 and it stops saying anything at all. That test was proved by adding the Master
Scent List to the `CAUTION` table and watching it fail on exactly that line.

## It glitched on the way out, and why

The owner reported it precisely, as they always do: *"something that happens is that the
window of the notes, when you click away, it glitches slightly."* It was real, and it was
two faults stacked.

**The window is built on the `<body>`, and it has to be** — a `position: fixed` element
inside an ancestor carrying a `transform` is positioned against that ancestor rather than
against the window, and a part's own box is given a transform while it opens. That makes
the window a **direct child of body**.

Which is exactly what this matches:

```css
body > *:not(.menu-trigger):not(.menu-overlay):not(.cursor-ring):not(.cursor-dot)
```

— the rule that dims the page while the Menu is open. A child selector with four
`:not()` on it outranks a plain `.note-panel`, so **the window never had the transition
it was written with**. Its own rule says `opacity 300ms, transform 300ms`; what it
actually got was the menu's `opacity 0.85s` — no transform at all, so it never slid or
scaled, it only faded, and it took nearly three times as long to do it. The scrim was
caught the same way.

**And the script was hiding it on a timer.** `close()` set a `setTimeout` for
`SHUT_MS + 40` — 260ms. Two things were wrong with that and either alone would have been
enough: 220 was never the right number even against the 300ms the stylesheet intended,
and **the fade does not start when the timer does** — a click has to be handled and the
styles recalculated first, which on this page is another 80ms or so.

So the window was hidden 180ms into an 850ms fade. Measured, frame by frame: the panel
and **the scrim with it** went from **0.606 opacity to nothing in a single frame**. The
scrim is a grey wash over the whole page, so what the owner saw was the entire window
flash.

**Both halves are fixed.** The two selectors now exclude `.note-panel` and `.note-scrim`,
so the window has its own 300ms and its transform back — and it is over the menu overlay
anyway (z-index 91 against 25), so there was never anything for the dimming to do to it.
And `close()` **waits for the fade itself** rather than for a number that has to be kept
in step with the stylesheet by hand: it listens for `transitionend` on the panel's own
opacity, with the timer left as a backstop for the case where the transition never runs
at all. Measured again, it now fades to 0.002 and *then* goes — and the whole close takes
365ms rather than 950.

**The listener has to be taken off again.** A window reopened before it had finished
going would otherwise be settled shut by the fade it was already half way through, so
`open()` and `settle()` both clear it.

## How to test it

```bash
npm test -- tests/notes.spec.js
```

Twenty-four tests, and **the seven that matter most do not open a browser at all**.
Eighty-odd hand-gathered entries can be quietly wrong in ways nobody would notice, so:
no entry is both a pyramid and a flat list (which would claim both that the source
divided the notes and that it did not); a `missing` entry carries a sentence and no
lists; every entry names a source with an `https` link, with one written-down exemption;
no entry is empty; a second list names itself and its own source; a landscape comes from
the house and nowhere else; a version names a year; and **every key points at a part that
really exists on its page**, which is the notes' end of the renumbering trap.

The rest: the Fragrances view being its own page and pointing at no house; the button
appearing on every fragrance and opening a window; a flat list saying it is undivided and
showing one row; a missing one saying so; and no button at all with the script blocked.

**Seven tests were added this round and every one of them was proved against a real fault
before being trusted**, which is the rule here:

- **`Almost Human carries a landscape before its notes`** — with the landscape window
  built after the notes window, the buttons read *View notes, Olfactory landscape* and it
  fails. With the one-at-a-time guard in `open()` taken out, two windows stand together
  and it fails on that instead.
- **`a reformulated fragrance says which version, at the top`** — with the version line
  moved below the notes the window reads *note-pyramid → note-version → note-source*, and
  it fails on the ordering.
- **`Haxan carries the perfumer's account and the fallback's reading`** — with the second
  list taken out, it fails.
- **`a landscape comes from the house and nowhere else`** — with one landscape pointed at
  Fragrantica, it fails.
- **`a fragrance with more than one version says which one`** — with a version written as
  "the new one" instead of a year, it fails.
- **`a fragrance whose source says nothing says so, in its own words`** — with the entry
  dropped altogether the window falls back to "have not been found yet", which is a
  different claim, and it fails.
- **`only the fragrances with a landscape have the button`** — Almost Human is the only
  house that publishes one, and a button on a fragrance with nothing behind it would open
  an empty window.

Three more for the window: the notes collapsing with the fragrance **and not coming back
with it**; the window being really *over* the page (on the body, fixed, centred on the
viewport, over a scrim, with the page held and unpressable); and it closing three ways —
the scrim, escape, and its own close. On a phone the same two run again at 390px, where
what is checked is that it **fits**: a 430px window on a 390px screen would hang off both
sides, and a window that fitted by being a thin strip would be no use either.

Two more for the close, and they are the two halves of the glitch above. **`the window
fades all the way out rather than being cut off`** watches the scrim's opacity every
frame from the click until it is hidden and keeps the last one: with the old timer it
reads **0.506 the frame before it vanished**, and fails. **`the window keeps its own
transition, not the menu's`** is the root rather than the symptom, and it is the half
that will come back — anything added as a child of body, or any change to the menu's
dimming rule, can quietly take the window's arrival away again. With the exclusions
removed it fails.

Two more for the hierarchy, and both were proved against a real fault before being
trusted. **Every source is either a house's own page or the fallback**: a source named
Fragrantica really links to `fragrantica.com` and one named for a house really does not,
which catches a link pasted under the wrong name — proved by pointing one Master Scent
List entry at Fragrantica and watching it fail. And **the fallback carries a warning and
a house source does not**, which opens one of each and checks the caution's text, that it
is hidden until hovered, and that it is not there at all on the house-sourced one.

## Known issues / TODO

- **Three fragrances were looked up and came back with nothing**, and say so: ADAR's Root
  Code and Lithos Diaphanes, and Grande Parfums' "Cookie something (?)". Two of those are
  waiting on ADAR to print a list; the third is waiting on the owner to settle the name.
  The empty slot at Individual 05 is deliberate and theirs.

- **The notes need JavaScript.** Everything else on a house page is readable without it;
  this is the one part that is not. It is reference beside the reading rather than the
  reading, which is why that was thought acceptable — but it is a real exception to the
  site's rule and worth revisiting if the list ever stops growing.
- **The sources are unreachable from this environment**, so almost nothing here was read
  at its source — `fragrantica.com`, `pinewardperfume.com`, `adarperfumes.com`,
  `grandeperfumes.com` and `almosthuman.store` all answer 403 to CONNECT at the gateway,
  and everything came through web search summaries of those pages instead. **The one
  exception is Haxan's second list**, which the owner screenshotted for me. Anything that
  matters should be checked against the link in its entry.
- **The window being a child of `body` is a standing hazard.** It has to be there, and
  being there puts it in reach of any rule written as `body > *`. There is one such rule
  on the site and the window is now excluded from it; a second would catch it again. The
  test above is the guard.
- **`window.HOUSE_NOTES` is a third thing to set** when a house is added, beside the
  search manifest and the contact sheet. Ataraxia and Les Abstraits deliberately do not
  set it: their fragrances have no names yet, so there is nothing to look up.
