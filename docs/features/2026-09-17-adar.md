# ADAR

Date: 2026-09-17 (introduced in `d7092b2`; its photographs and the mark in the void in
`2dd0dc1`; migrated from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-17)

Files: `houses/adar.html`, `adar.js` (~820 lines), the `adar-*` block in `style.css`,
`images/ADAR/`, `tests/adar.spec.js`

The second house in Scent descriptions, "the house that you have never heard of".

## What it is

The same shape as [Pineward](2026-09-16-pineward.md) on a different ground: an introduction and
then the fragrances, each compacted to a number, a small picture and a title until it
is opened. Where Pineward has a wood, this has a **void**.

The owner asked for it to be "mysterious … unknown and void-y", and what that means
here is: near-white on near-black, a hole standing off to one side of the window with
soundings ringing out from it, and **no colour at all**. `tests/adar.spec.js` reads
the whole canvas and fails on anything that is not grey, and reads the writing over it
for the site's accent too.

## The eleven

Eleven fragrances in four groups — three trilogies (Aegis, Équilibre Précaire,
Introspection) and the two that stand outside them — written in the page's own markup
(`<section class="adar-group">`), not worked out in the script. They are numbered
straight through, `part-01` to `part-11`.

A fragrance is a `<details class="adar-part">`: a number, a small picture and a title
in its `<summary>`, the full picture and the writing inside. A **stage** — top, mid,
base, a sidenote — is a `<p class="adar-stage">` label above the paragraphs it belongs
to, because that is how the owner writes.

**ADAR Effect™ is the owner's own coinage** for this house's turpentine quality — the
menthol-like trigeminal lift without the dense forest behind it. It is deliberately
*not* written that way in three places: the introduction, which says "it has a DNA for
sure", and the two entries that say "ADAR DNA" and mean it. Don't tidy those.

## The void

- **The void is a hole, not a disc drawn over the drawing.** Everything is drawn, and
  then the disc is taken back out of the finished drawing with `destination-out`. With
  that operation it is the **alpha of the fill** that says how much is taken out, and
  whatever was last used to draw a speck is a few hundredths of one — left as it was,
  the hole came out as a smudge with the drawing still faintly in it. Set the fill
  solid before clearing; a test reads the middle of the void and requires nothing at
  all there.
- **The hole needs something to be a hole in.** The page's ground and the inside of the
  void are the same near-black, so a **halo** is stamped round it (`stampHalo`) — one
  radial gradient drawn once into a canvas of its own and then `drawImage`d, the way
  [the structure](2026-09-14-the-structure.md) stamps its glows rather than asking for a fresh
  gradient every frame.
- **The void shows the house's mark, under the pointer only.**
  `images/ADAR/adar-sigil.jpg` is drawn INSIDE the hole and nowhere else, clipped to
  the void's own circle and brought up only while the hand is within `LIGHT_IN` of its
  rim — a spotlight carried over something already down there rather than a picture
  hung on the page. It is brightest in the middle of the hole and eases off towards the
  edge, and if the file is not there the void simply stays empty.
- **The mark is lifted off its own ground rather than drawn as the picture it is**
  (`liftMark`). The file is a white mark on a black field, and two things went wrong
  with drawing it as it stands. Its black is a *compressed* black — a few parts in a
  hundred rather than none — and a few parts in a hundred of near-white over a
  near-black hole is a visible rectangle standing in the middle of the void, which is
  what it came out as. And most of that file is field: fitted whole, the lettering came
  out a third the size the hole could hold. So it is stamped once into a canvas of its
  own with its brightness as its ALPHA (below `SIGIL_FLOOR` it is nothing at all) and
  the page's own ink as its colour, and then **cut down to its own ink** — after which
  what is drawn is the lettering and nothing else, with the hole showing through round
  it. Stamped once and drawn many times, the same way the halo is. It is also **stood
  inside** the hole (`SIGIL_FILL`) rather than filled to it: filled, a mark wider or
  taller than it is square has its ends cropped away by the very rim it is meant to
  stand in.
- **The rings are carried by the scroll and the specks fall on the clock**, both worked
  out fresh every frame rather than added up, so travelling back gets you back to the
  same drawing. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the clock stops and the void simply
  stands there.
- **The specks near the rim are drawn as short arcs** along the way they are
  travelling, and as squares further out — what is falling round a hole is not standing
  still.
- **The middle of the window is kept quiet** (`CLEAR_MID`), where the writing stands,
  but never emptied: a speck there is left at `CLEAR_INK` rather than taken out, because
  a column of nothing down the middle of a drawing is the **invisible pane**
  [the chamber](2026-09-15-the-chamber.md) learnt not to stand in its own.

## The left margin: the log and the dust

The right of the window has the void and the left had nothing, which the owner asked
to have filled, subtly: a hairline ruled down it, ticked like a depth scale and
travelling with the page at its own rate (`LOG_*`), with a slow fall of specks through
it (`DUST_*`). Instrument marks rather than a picture, at a tenth of the ink the void's
rings get.

## The sounding, and the reading

**The sounding** is the scale down the side: one tick per fragrance, inked in as it is
passed. It is Pineward's **trunk** by another name, and it reads the PARTS rather than
the scrollbar for the same reason — a fragrance that runs long should not read as more
of the house than one that runs short.

**The reading in the corner names WHAT FILLS THE WINDOW**, not what was passed last
(`filling`, `mostOf`). At the foot of a long open fragrance the thing you are looking
at began a long way up, so where something starts says nothing; how much of the screen
it has says everything. And when an open fragrance is most of what you can see — more
than `NAMES_IT` (0.45) of the window — the reading names it as well as its group:
"Aegis Trilogy, Amber Zero". The owner asked for both. Pineward's reading works the
same way, and is worth keeping in step.

## Opening a fragrance

A fragrance opens as a movement, not a cut — `OPEN_MS` (760ms) and `SHUT_MS` (620ms)
on `PART_EASE`. This is the same behaviour Pineward has, from the same code written
twice: [see Pineward](2026-09-16-pineward.md#opening-and-closing-a-part) for what makes it work.
These pages share nothing by design.

## The photographs

**Every fragrance names the photograph it wants** — `amber-zero.png` and so on, listed
in `images/README.txt` — and shows it the moment that file is in `images/ADAR/`. **All
eleven are there now**, and the house's mark with them; this page is the one place on
the site that is not waiting for a picture.

The fallback stays wired up: a picture that is not there is taken off the page and the
hatched placeholder stands in its place, so a house whose photographs are still coming
looks unfinished rather than broken.

The small picture in the list is cropped to a SQUARE round its subject — `--focus` says
which part to keep, and each of the eleven is set on its own bottle rather than on one
shared number. Opened, a picture keeps its own shape. ADHD carries three, and the
second and third stand inside the body, which is what makes them appear only once it is
open.

## How to test it

`tests/adar.spec.js`: the house being eleven fragrances in four groups numbered
straight through; a fragrance being a title until it is opened and the owner's own
writing once it is; the sounding counting them off as they are passed; the void being a
hole with nothing at all drawn inside it; the house's mark standing in that hole under
the hand with nothing of its own ground coming with it; every one of the eleven
carrying the photographs it asks for; no part of the page being drawn in the site's
accent colour; it standing still under `prefers-reduced-motion`; all of its writing
being there without its script. `tests/contact-sheet.spec.js` checks the sheet's second
picture points at it.

## A click that lands mid-movement is remembered

Opening a fragrance is a movement (`OPEN_MS`, 760ms) and closing it another
(`SHUT_MS`, 620ms), and while either was running the summary's click handler returned
without doing anything. So opening a part and changing your mind within the next
three-quarters of a second **swallowed the click** — nothing happened and you had to
click again.

It is remembered now instead: the click sets a flag, and the movement that is running
carries it out when it finishes. Only **one** is kept, so hammering the summary does at
most one more thing rather than queueing up a pile of toggles. `pineward.js` had the same
handler and the same bug, and has the same fix — the two houses are worth keeping in
step.

**This was found through a flaky test, and is worth the story.**
`a fragrance is a title until it is opened` failed about one full run in three and passed
every time on its own. The temptation was to write it off as timing on a loaded machine.
It was not: the test clicks to open, waits for the writing to be visible — which happens
early in the 760ms — and clicks again to close. Under load that second click landed
inside the animation and was dropped, so the part stayed open and the test was right to
fail. A test that only fails when the machine is busy can still be reporting a real bug;
this one was reporting a swallowed click that any reader could hit.

## The sounding has to reach its end

The owner, with a screenshot of the page scrolled right to the bottom: the scale still
read short of eleven. Pineward had exactly this and was fixed a round earlier; this is
the same fault and the same rule, and the two pages are worth keeping in step.

A fragrance counts as passed when its top crosses a line **a third of the way down the
window**. The last few never get that far up the screen — the page runs out before they
can — so scrolling to the very bottom left the sounding short of its end. The fix is a
second rule rather than a different line: **once the page itself has been passed, so has
everything on it.** When the scroll is at its maximum, every fragrance counts.
